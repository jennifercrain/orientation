Peer Navigator Training

Success Tools

Help students identify what’s getting in the way and take a next step they can use.

Coaching With Success Tools

Success Tools help students work through common barriers to academic progress, such as low motivation, unclear goals, setbacks, and competing demands. Start by listening to how the student describes the situation. Ask a focused question that helps both of you understand what’s getting in the way.

You can offer a strategy or share a practical tip. Make it coaching by helping the student put the idea into action. Ask how they’d apply it, what they’d change to make it fit, and what they’ll do first. When possible, have them try that first step during the conversation.

Example. After introducing time blocking, ask “Where could you realistically fit a study block this week?” and “What will you work on during that block?” Then invite the student to add it to their calendar using the task as the title.

Coaching Expectations

Explore before giving advice

Ask what the student has already tried and what happened. After offering a tip, ask how they’d apply it and help them identify a specific next action.

Stay within your role

Do not diagnose or label students. Reflect what they describe in their own words, and keep the conversation focused on coursework and support within the Peer Navigator role.

Check your assumptions

Ask what the student means before deciding what’s causing the problem. “When you say you can’t get started, what happens when you sit down to study?”

Keep the focus on the student

Share a brief personal example when it helps. Then return to the student’s experience. “What part of that, if any, could work for you?”

Make encouragement specific

Avoid shallow reassurance or cheerleading. Name an effort or a strength the student has shown, then ask how they could build on it. “Short study sessions helped you finish the last unit. What could you reuse here?”

Be warm and professional

Use respectful language and take the concern seriously. Avoid jokes or comments that minimize what the student shares. Give the student time to respond in their own words.

Explore the Topics

“I don’t know where to start” could reflect unclear priorities, low energy, or uncertainty after a setback. Ask what happens when the student tries to start, then choose a topic based on what they share.

Burnout & Motivation

“I know what I need to do. I just can’t make myself start.”

Goal Setting

“I’m working, but I don’t feel like I’m making progress.”

Mindset

“I’m just not good at this.”

Resilience

“I fell off track and don’t know how to get back.”

Time Management

“I’m busy all day and still feel behind.”
