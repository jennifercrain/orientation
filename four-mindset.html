Success Tools

Mindset

Help students examine what a difficult experience means to them and choose a response they can use.

About Mindset

Students’ interpretations of effort and setbacks can shape whether they stay engaged. A difficult quiz, an unfamiliar topic, or a mistake can lead a student to question their ability. A coaching conversation can help them describe the specific difficulty and recognize evidence they can build on.

What You Might Hear

  • “I’m just not good at this.”
  • “Everyone else seems to get it but me.”
  • “If I can’t do it perfectly, I shouldn’t do it at all.”

Coaching Technique

Reflect the concern and ask for specifics

Acknowledge the experience, then ask what happened and which part was difficult. Use the student’s own words and look together at something concrete, such as a quiz item or rubric criterion.

What to watch for. Avoid arguing with how the student feels, diagnosing their thinking, or offering generic reassurance. Be warm and professional. Give the student room to find a realistic interpretation they can believe.

Strategies to Try

Growth-Oriented Reframing

Use this when a student interprets a difficult experience as evidence about their overall ability. Help them name the specific skill they’re still learning, then choose one way to practice it.

Ask. “Looking at the questions you missed, what’s one skill you want to practice next?”

Confidence Through Evidence

Ask the student to recall a similar challenge they handled and identify what helped. Have them choose one part of that approach to reuse in the current course.

Ask. “When have you worked through something like this before? What could you use again here?”

Coaching in Action

Student. “I missed half the questions. I’m just bad at this.”

Peer Navigator. “Missing that many felt discouraging. Looking at the questions, where did you feel least sure?”

Student. “I could define the terms. I got mixed up when I had to apply them to scenarios.”

Peer Navigator. “Knowing the definitions gives you something to build on. How would you describe what you need to practice next?”

Student. “I need to practice spotting which term fits a scenario.”

Peer Navigator. “Pick one missed scenario. What clue could you look for as you try it again?”

Student. “I’ll look for what changed in the scenario. That helps me narrow down which term fits.”

What the student did. The student moved from a broad judgment about their ability to a specific skill they could practice with a missed question.
