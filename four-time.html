Success Tools

Time Management

Help students plan coursework around the time and attention they realistically have.

About Time Management

Work, family, and other commitments can compete with study time. Even when students find a window to work, vague tasks, inaccurate time estimates, or interruptions can make it hard to use that time. Ask where the plan breaks down before choosing a tool.

What You Might Hear

  • “I’m busy all day and still feel behind.”
  • “I sit down to work, but nothing gets done.”
  • “Everything feels urgent.”

Coaching Technique

Look at a real week together

Ask the student to describe a recent study attempt or open their calendar. Check how much time they actually have, what they planned to do, and what interrupted them. Choose a strategy that addresses where the plan breaks down.

What to watch for. Avoid prescribing your own study schedule or treating every time problem as a motivation problem. Keep the plan flexible enough for the student’s existing responsibilities.

Strategies to Try

Time Blocking

Pair a realistic window with one specific task. Have the student add the block to their calendar and name the task in the event title.

Ask. “Which study window can you protect this week, and what will you work on during it?”

Task Duration Estimation

If tasks keep running long, have the student compare a new task with one they’ve completed. Ask them to estimate the time and decide what portion fits their next window.

Ask. “How long did a similar task take? What part can you realistically finish in the time you have?”

Distraction Planning

Help the student identify a likely interruption and choose how to handle it. Ask what they need to stay available for and what can wait until the study block ends.

Ask. “What’s likely to pull your attention away, and what will you do when it happens?”

Coaching in Action

Student. “I keep putting ‘study’ on my calendar, but when the time comes, I don’t know what to work on.”

Peer Navigator. “Which study window is most likely to work this week, and how long is it?”

Student. “Wednesday after dinner. I have about 30 minutes.”

Peer Navigator. “A time block can pair that window with a specific task. What course task could fit in those 30 minutes?”

Student. “I can do the first five practice questions and mark the ones I want to review.”

Peer Navigator. “Add that as the title of your Wednesday block. What could help you start when the block begins?”

Student. “I added it. I’ll leave the practice questions open so I can go straight to them.”

What the student did. The student applied Time Blocking in their own calendar by pairing a realistic study window with a specific task.
