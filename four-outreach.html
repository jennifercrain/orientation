Proactively connect with students and build meaningful support relationships

Outreach Calling

Outreach calling, sometimes called care calling, is an opportunity to connect with students, build rapport, and offer support before they feel disengaged.

Students may be surprised by an outreach call because they didn’t schedule it. Keep the conversation supportive and low-pressure. Lead with curiosity and encouragement, and connect students to resources that can help.

Goals of Outreach

  • Understand how the student is doing.
  • Identify possible barriers or challenges.
  • Connect the student to resources or strategies.
  • Encourage continued engagement with their course and support systems.

The purpose of outreach is to provide proactive support, increase awareness of peer services, and offer bite-sized coaching.

The goal is not to turn the call into an appointment. Outreach calls are unscheduled and take place outside our scheduling system, so they don’t count as appointments in our data. Help students feel supported and encourage them to schedule an appointment or attend a live event, whichever fits their needs.

Beginning the Call

Place outreach calls through Genesys Cloud using the Outreach Call List on the appropriate course SharePoint site.

If the Student Does Not Answer

Leave a brief, professional voicemail and follow up by email.

“Hi, this is [Name] from WGU. I’m going to send a follow-up email, and I hope we can reconnect another time. Thank you!”

Note: Unlike appointments, do not provide a call-back if the student does not answer the call the first time. Complete your documentation and move to the next student.

If the Student Answers

Introduce yourself, explain your role, and ask whether it’s a good time to talk.

“Hi [Student Name]! This is [Your Name], and I’m a Peer Navigator at WGU. I was hoping we could chat for a few minutes. Is now a good time?”

If now isn’t a good time, offer to follow up by email.

“No problem! I’ll send a follow-up email in hopes that we can connect another time. Have a great day!”

Verify the student’s identity before discussing student information. Request their:

  • First and last name
  • Program Mentor’s name
  • Student ID number, or their WGU username if they can’t access their student ID

Leading the Conversation

After verification, explain why you’re calling and ask how the student is doing in the course. Help them reflect on what’s challenging and identify what would help them continue.

Acknowledge that a course can feel challenging, then help the student identify a manageable next step.

Example Questions

  • “How is [Course] going for you?”
  • “Is there anything in the course that’s been feeling challenging lately?”
  • “What’s been the hardest thing to juggle with school right now?”
  • “Has anything surprised you about how this course is going?”
  • “What kind of support would make the biggest difference for you this week?”

Connecting Students to Support

Once you identify a challenge or concern, introduce a strategy, resource, or support service that may help the student move forward.

Resources to Consider

  • Course resources or cohorts
  • Academic Coaching Center articles or services
  • Live events
  • Peer Navigator appointments
  • Program Mentors
  • Student Services
  • Student Wellbeing Services

What to Explain

  • What the resource is
  • How it may help with the student’s challenge
  • How the student can access it

Keep strategy discussions brief and approachable. Use the call to introduce support and encourage engagement. For more detailed practice or guidance, invite the student to a live event or help them schedule an appointment.

Inviting Students to Reconnect

For Appointment-Based Interns

Encourage the student to schedule a session for individualized support.

“I also offer Peer Navigator appointments where we can walk through study strategies, explore resources together, or even just check in as accountability partners for this course.”
“Would you be interested in scheduling time to reconnect?”

If they’re interested, try to schedule the appointment during the call.

For Live Event Interns

Personally invite the student to an upcoming live event and explain how it could help with their goals or challenges.

“My next live event is coming up on [Date and Time], and we’ll be talking about [Topic].”
“We’re going to focus on [Strategy] and how it can help make that section of the course easier to break down.”

If they can’t attend, share a general appointment link or suggest other support options.

Closing the Call

Check for Questions

“Before we wrap up, do you have any questions for me?”
“Is there anything else I can help support you with today?”

Thank the Student and Keep the Door Open

“Thank you so much for your time. It was really great talking with you.”
“If you need anything, feel free to reply to my email and we’ll make sure you get connected to the right resources.”

Let the student know their follow-up email will include:

  • Relevant resources
  • Scheduling links or live event information
  • Additional support information discussed during the call

Outreach Documentation and Procedures

After each outreach interaction, complete the required documentation and follow-up tasks.

After Each Outreach Interaction

  • Update the Outreach Call List as needed.
  • Send the follow-up email and BCC your leader.
  • Complete the Outreach-Specific Student Note Form for each individual student contacted.
  • Complete the Interaction Data Form with a cumulative number (total) of calls made per course at the end of each shift.

Follow-Up Email Expectations

  • Personalize the email with the student note, course code, and other relevant details as needed.
  • Use professional formatting in Arial, size 14.
  • Follow the team’s email expectations.

Interaction Data reminder. Log outreach interactions as Outreach Call and FLUP Email, as applicable. Use Email Exchanges only for transactional back-and-forth email communication with students.
