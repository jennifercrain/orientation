Week Four

Burnout & Motivation

Explore what makes it hard to start or sustain coursework, then help the student choose a manageable next step.

About Burnout & Motivation

A student can understand what they need to do and still have trouble starting or following through. Low energy, competing demands, an overwhelming task, or uncertainty about why the work matters can each shape what feels doable.

What You Might Hear

  • “I know what I need to do. I just can’t make myself start.”
  • “I feel so behind that I don’t see the point anymore.”
  • “By the time I sit down to study, I’m already exhausted.”

Coaching Technique

Ask what happens at the point of starting

Reflect what the student shared. Ask them to walk you through the last time they planned to study and listen for what got in the way. Check whether the barrier is the size of the task, the energy they have at study time, or something else.

What to watch for. Avoid labeling the student as burned out or unmotivated. Take what they describe seriously. Skip pep talks and ask what would make the next attempt feel manageable.

Strategies to Try

Micro-Goal Reset

Use this when the student feels overwhelmed by the size of a task. Open the task together, break it into smaller pieces, and ask the student to choose one piece they can start now.

Ask. “Which part could you make smaller? What could you do in the next five minutes?”

Energy-Based Planning

Use this when the student repeatedly tries to study at a time when they have little energy. Look at a typical week together and have them pick an available window and a task that fits it.

Ask. “When do you usually have enough energy to focus? What task could you try during one of those windows?”

Coaching in Action

Student. “I open Task 2, see all the requirements, and close it again.”

Peer Navigator. “Seeing the whole task at once sounds overwhelming. Which part feels hardest to begin?”

Student. “The first section. I don’t know what to put in it.”

Peer Navigator. “Would it help to break that section down? Open the rubric with me. What’s one thing the first criterion asks you to do?”

Student. “Identify a stakeholder. I can list two possibilities now and choose one when I sit down tomorrow.”

Peer Navigator. “You’ve got two possibilities on the page now. When tomorrow will you choose one?”

Student. “At 7, after dinner. I’ll pick one before I draft anything.”

What the student did. The student tried a Micro-Goal Reset by opening the rubric, identifying one requirement, and writing down two possibilities during the session.
