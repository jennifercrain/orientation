Week Four

Goal Setting

Help students choose a priority and define what progress will look like.

About Goal Setting

A student may be putting in effort and still feel scattered when several tasks compete for attention. Goal-setting conversations help them decide what matters now, define an achievable outcome, and choose a point to check progress.

What You Might Hear

  • “I’m working, but I don’t feel like I’m making progress.”
  • “I’m not sure if I’m focusing on the right things.”
  • “I have so much to do that I don’t know where to start.”

Coaching Technique

Move from a broad intention to a clear finish line

Ask which outcome matters most before the next deadline or check-in. Help the student choose a realistic priority, define what completion looks like, and decide when to check their progress.

What to watch for. Avoid choosing the goal or deadline for the student. Ask what feels realistic given their time and workload. Keep your attention on the student’s situation if you share a brief example from your own experience.

Strategies to Try

Prioritization & Focus

Use this when several tasks compete for attention. Have the student name their next milestone, compare the tasks against it, and choose what to work on first and what can wait.

Ask. “Which task would move you closest to your next milestone? What can you set aside for now?”

SMART Goals

Help the student make a goal specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound. Ask them to write the goal in their own words and check it against their available time.

Ask. “What will you complete, how will you know it’s done, and when can you realistically finish?”

Coaching in Action

Student. “My goal is to get caught up this week.”

Peer Navigator. “What would feel like meaningful progress by the end of the week?”

Student. “Getting the first section of Task 1 drafted.”

Peer Navigator. “How will you know the draft is ready to review, and when could you finish it?”

Student. “I’ll have a paragraph for each of the three rubric criteria by Thursday evening.”

Peer Navigator. “Let’s write that in your plan. Looking at the rubric now, which criterion will you start with?”

Student. “The first one asks me to describe the situation. I’ll put a few bullets under that heading now.”

What the student did. The student chose the goal, defined what completion looks like, and identified a first action they could take during the appointment.
