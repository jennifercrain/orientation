Week Four

Resilience

Help students restart after a setback and adapt their plan to what they can do now.

About Resilience

A missed deadline, an unsuccessful assessment attempt, or a break in coursework can interrupt a student’s momentum. Resilience coaching gives the student room to acknowledge what happened, identify what helped them recover before, and choose a realistic way back into the course.

What You Might Hear

  • “I fell off track and don’t know how to get back.”
  • “I failed my OA, so I’m probably too far behind now.”
  • “One bad week turns into no progress at all.”

Coaching Technique

Find a realistic point of re-entry

Ask where the student left off, what has changed, and what is available to them now. Acknowledge the disruption and give the student room to choose a way back into coursework that fits their circumstances.

What to watch for. Avoid minimizing the setback or pushing the student to make up everything at once. Stay within the Peer Navigator role when discussing assessment results and course requirements. Help the student identify who can clarify requirements when they need that information.

Strategies to Try

Setback Recovery

After a disappointment, help the student describe what happened without judgment. Use any feedback or information they have to identify one realistic action and decide when to try it.

Ask. “What does the feedback point you toward? Which part could you revisit first?”

Return-from-Break Planning

When the student has been away from the course, open it together and find where they left off. Have them choose a small re-entry task, such as reviewing their last page of notes, and decide when to begin.

Ask. “Where did you stop, and what’s one small step that would help you pick it back up?”

Coaching in Action

Student. “I haven’t touched the course in a week. I feel like I have to start from scratch.”

Peer Navigator. “A week away can make it hard to know where to pick up. Where were you when you stopped?”

Student. “I was halfway through Unit 2. I have notes, but I’m not sure what I remember.”

Peer Navigator. “What would be a low-pressure way to see what’s still familiar?”

Student. “I can skim my Unit 2 notes and mark anything I don’t remember.”

Peer Navigator. “Let’s open them and try that with the first page. What would tell you where to pick up next?”

Student. “I recognize the first two examples. I marked the third one, so I’ll start there tomorrow.”

What the student did. The student practiced restarting with work they had already done and used that review to identify where to continue.
